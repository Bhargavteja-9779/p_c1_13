We submit our Original Research article, "Apparent Gains in Detecting Glaucomatous Visual-Field Progression Disappear at Matched False-Alarm Rates: A Two-Stage Benchmark", for consideration in *Ophthalmology and Therapy*.

Decisions to escalate glaucoma treatment depend on detecting visual-field progression. Many new detectors, including machine-learning models, claim to detect progression earlier than a simple trend analysis, but they are rarely compared at the same false-alarm rate. A false alarm can lead to unnecessary tests, drops, laser or surgery.

In two pre-specified studies using three public data sets, we compared trend, permutation, event-based, supervised and self-supervised machine-learning detectors. We measured false alarms on retest series in which glaucoma could not have changed, and in Study 2 we held every detector to the same false-alarm rate with a simple procedure ("shuffle calibration") that any clinic can apply to its own records.

The main findings are:

- A machine-learning detector thresholded in the usual way appeared 22.0 percentage points more sensitive than the trend test, but raised false alarms in 15.3% of true no-change series.
- At matched false alarms, no detector was significantly more sensitive than a plain trend test of mean sensitivity, across 150 progressing and 164 stable eyes; gains of 8.4 percentage points or more were excluded.
- Over repeated reviews, controlling cumulative false alarms to 5% reduced detection by the sixth test from 64.7% to 47.6%, a trade-off that clinicians can now choose explicitly.

We believe these results will help clinicians judge claims of earlier detection and help developers and regulators evaluate new detectors fairly. Every number in the manuscript is generated by script from the result files; code and raw outputs are available on request.
