We submit our Original Research article, "How Early Can Glaucomatous Visual-Field Progression Be Detected Without False Alarms? An Open Benchmark With a True No-Change Arm", for consideration in *Ophthalmology and Therapy*.

Decisions to escalate glaucoma treatment depend on detecting visual-field progression. Many new detectors claim to find progression earlier, but their false-alarm rates are rarely measured on data in which no change occurred. A false alarm can lead to unnecessary drops, laser or surgery.

We compared ten pre-specified detectors, from simple trend tests to permutation, conformal, event-based and machine-learning methods, using only the first three to six tests of each series. We measured sensitivity against a future-confirmed reference, false alarms on 12-week retest series in which glaucoma could not have changed, and agreement with clinician labels in an external clinic in China.

The main findings are:

- No detector with a valid false-alarm rate was significantly more sensitive than a simple trend test of mean sensitivity.
- The only significantly more sensitive detector raised false alarms in 22.4% of true no-change series, and a machine-learning model thresholded at 95% specificity raised false alarms in 27.7%.
- A simple "shuffle audit", which any clinic can run on its own records, flagged the same invalid detectors.

We believe these results are useful to clinicians who must judge claims of earlier detection, and to developers of new detectors. The analysis plan was time-stamped before the final analysis, all data are public, and all code and raw outputs will be released. Every number in the manuscript is generated by script from the result files.
