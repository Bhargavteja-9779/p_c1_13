Dear Editor,

Please consider our Original Research article, "How Early Can Glaucoma Visual-Field Progression Be Detected Without False Alarms? An Open Benchmark With a True No-Change Arm", for publication in *Ophthalmology and Therapy*.

Clinicians escalate glaucoma treatment when the visual field gets worse. Many new methods, including machine-learning models, claim to detect this earlier. Few have been checked on eyes that truly did not change, and a false progression flag can lead to unnecessary drops, laser or surgery.

We compared ten detectors in three public data sets, following an analysis plan committed before the final run. The benchmark includes a true no-change arm (patients tested weekly for 12 weeks), a future-confirmed reference and an external clinic in China. With three tests, the most sensitive conformal and machine-learning detectors raised false alarms on 22.4% and 27.7% of true no-change series, although they looked as specific as the simple trend test on reference-stable eyes. Even the simple trend test flagged 12.6% of no-change series at least once over four reviews. We report all results, including negative ones, the methods we developed and abandoned, and every deviation from the analysis plan.

We believe the study fits the journal's interest in real-world evidence on decisions about glaucoma therapy. Its practical message is that a new detector should not drive treatment escalation until its false-alarm rate on true no-change data is known. All code, per-eye outputs and result files are provided.

The manuscript has not been published and is not under consideration elsewhere. Both authors approved the submission. No funding was received, and the authors declare no competing interests. The use of an AI-based assistant for code and drafting is disclosed in the manuscript.
