Dear Editor,

Please consider our Original Research article, "How Early Can Glaucoma Visual-Field Progression Be Detected Without False Alarms? An Open Benchmark With a True No-Change Arm", for publication in *Ophthalmology and Therapy*.

Clinicians escalate glaucoma treatment when the visual field gets worse. Many new methods, including machine-learning models, claim to detect this earlier. Few have been checked on eyes that truly did not change. A false progression flag can lead to unnecessary drops, laser or surgery.

We compared ten detectors in three public data sets, with a pre-registered analysis plan. The benchmark includes a true no-change arm (patients tested weekly for 12 weeks), a future-confirmed reference and an external clinic in China. With three tests, the most sensitive conformal and machine-learning detectors raised false alarms on 22.4% and 27.7% of true no-change series, although they looked as specific as the simple trend test on reference-stable eyes. No detector with a valid false-alarm rate flagged significantly more progressors than the trend of mean sensitivity. We also report negative results, including the methods we developed and abandoned.

We believe the study fits the journal's interest in real-world evidence on decisions about glaucoma therapy. It gives clinicians a practical rule: do not let a new detector drive treatment escalation until its false-alarm rate on true no-change data is known. All code and result files are open, so others can add detectors to the benchmark.

The manuscript has not been published and is not under consideration elsewhere. All authors approved the submission. [AUTHORS TO CONFIRM: The authors declare no competing interests.] An AI assistant was used for code and drafting under the authors' direction; this is disclosed in the manuscript.
