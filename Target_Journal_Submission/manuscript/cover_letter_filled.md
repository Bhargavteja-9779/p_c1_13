We submit our Original Research article, "Detecting Glaucomatous Visual Field Progression From Few Tests Without False Alarms: A Benchmark With a True No-Change Arm", for consideration in *Ophthalmology and Therapy*.

Treatment of glaucoma is escalated when the visual field gets worse, and many new detectors, including machine-learning models, claim to detect this earlier. Most are judged by how many eyes they flag, so an earlier flag cannot be told apart from a false alarm. We compared ten detectors on the first three to six fields using a future-confirmed reference, a true no-change arm (patients tested weekly for 12 weeks), and an external data set from China measured with a different perimeter.

The main finding is practical. With three fields, a pooled-detrended conformal test and a machine-learning model flagged more progressors than a simple trend test (57% and 26% versus 19%) but raised false alarms in 22.4% and 27.7% of true no-change series, which specificity in reference-stable eyes did not reveal. No detector with acceptable false alarms was significantly more sensitive than the trend test, although our study could exclude only large gains. A "shuffle audit" that clinics can run on their own records picked out the grossly inflated detectors but also gave false warnings, so we present it as a screening check. With few fields, every method missed most progressing eyes. We think these results are relevant to the journal's readers because they bear directly on when a progression flag should lead to escalation of therapy.

The analysis plan was written and dated before the final test data were analysed, every deviation from the protocol, including analyses added after an internal review, is listed in the Methods, and the code and result files are available on request. Only public, de-identified data were used. The manuscript has not been published and is not under consideration elsewhere, and all authors have approved it.
