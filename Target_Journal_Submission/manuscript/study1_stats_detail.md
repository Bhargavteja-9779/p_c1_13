In Study 1, proportions are reported with 95% confidence intervals from 2,000 bootstrap resamples of patients, which accounts for two eyes per patient and repeated windows per patient. We compared each detector with D1 on the same eyes using the exact McNemar test and corrected p-values for the nine comparisons within each number of tests and endpoint with the Holm method. For stochastic detectors, the paired tests used each eye's majority flag across seeds. Confidence intervals for paired differences came from 2,000 bootstrap resamples of eyes. Because of the majority flag, paired differences can differ slightly from differences between the seed-averaged percentages in the tables. With {{d:ref_progressors.n_eyes:int}} progressors and nine comparisons, a sensitivity gain over D1 could reach Holm-adjusted significance only if at least {{x:mdd_eyes}} more progressors were flagged ({{x:mdd_pp}} percentage points). The protocol stated that validity would be judged by comparing the upper confidence limit of the false-alarm rate on true no-change data with 5%. Read literally (upper limit at or below 5%), this rule could not be met even by D1, whose false-alarm rate was close to the nominal 5%, because the true no-change arm has only 30 patients. Before the final results were available, the analysis plan in our draft Methods specified a working rule: the upper limit must not exceed 10% and the point estimate must not exceed 5% by more than sampling error. After the results were available, we made the second part operational as "the lower confidence limit is at or below 5%". We call a detector valid under this rule, and we report the strict literal rule as a sensitivity analysis. This sequence is a deviation from the protocol. To express false alarms in clinical units, we report the expected number of false progression flags per 1,000 stable eyes at one review. We also report, among reference progressors, the share flagged at least once by the sixth test, and, as a matching measure, the share of true no-change series flagged at least once across the looks at three to six tests (window starting at the first test). These cumulative measures, the strict validity rule, a confidence interval for the shuffle-audit correlation (resampling detector–test-number cells) and the GRAPE results for the PLR labels were added after internal review and were not in the protocol. Agreement between the shuffle audit and the true no-change false-alarm rate was summarised by the Spearman correlation across detector–test-number cells and by their absolute difference. Analyses used Python 3.13 with NumPy, SciPy, pandas, scikit-learn, LightGBM and PyTorch. The Study 1 run took {{m:runtime_min}} minutes on a 4-core CPU without a graphics card.
